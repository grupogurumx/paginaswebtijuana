Ceramic vs. porcelain tile: how to choose the right floor for every space

Absorption, strength, price and maintenance: the real differences between ceramic and porcelain tile, so you invest in the right surface.

It's the question we hear most in our showroom: which is better, ceramic or porcelain tile? The honest answer depends on the space, the use and the budget. They may look alike, but inside they are very different materials. Here are the differences that really matter.

What is ceramic tile?

Ceramic tile is made from clays pressed and fired at around 1,000 °C. Its body is more porous and is covered by a glaze that provides color and design. It is lighter, cuts easily and comes in a huge variety of colors, reliefs and formats.

What is porcelain tile?

Porcelain uses finer clays and feldspar, is pressed at higher pressure and fired above 1,200 °C. The result is a dense body with water absorption under 0.5%, far more resistant to bending, wear and temperature changes.

Quick comparison

FeatureCeramicPorcelain
Water absorption3% to 10%+Under 0.5%
Wear resistanceMediumHigh to very high
Outdoor useNot recommendedYes (anti-slip versions)
FormatsSmall and mediumUp to large format
Ease of cuttingHighNeeds professional tools
InvestmentLowerHigher, with longer life

When to choose ceramic

  • Bathroom and kitchen walls: no foot traffic, so ceramic performs perfectly.
  • Backsplashes and feature walls: where you want color, shine and handcrafted texture.
  • Budget-conscious projects in light-traffic areas.

When to choose porcelain

  • High-traffic floors: living rooms, hallways, retail, offices and restaurants.
  • Outdoors, terraces and pools: it withstands frost, sun and moisture.
  • Bathroom and shower floors, thanks to low absorption.
  • Large-format designs with minimal joints and marble or stone looks.

Our recommendation

A formula that works in most homes: porcelain on every floor and ceramic on bathroom and kitchen walls. You get durability where it's needed most and design freedom where it shows most.
